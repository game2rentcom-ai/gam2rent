# BharatPeX enters checkout; alternatives carry risk

BharatPe **can** technically be used for e-commerce website checkout through its subsidiary brand BharatPeX, which received RBI online Payment Aggregator approval in April 2025 and launched a full payment gateway at the Global Fintech Fest in September 2026. It supports cards, UPI, net banking, EMI, Buy Now Pay Later, and 100+ payment methods through a single API/SDK integration. However, BharatPeX is **not a practical self-serve option today**: there is no public developer documentation, no sandbox, no self-sign-up portal, and no known community adoption — integration requires going through an enterprise sales team. For gaming or digital goods merchants specifically, Cashfree is the only gateway among the major alternatives with a published, explicit policy that permits online gaming (non-money) with a legal opinion and RNG certificate; PayU's prohibited list is non-public; and EaseBuzz broadly supports digital goods but has no official gaming policy. All three face regulatory pressure following India's 2025 online money-gaming restrictions, and a business selling game account credentials specifically carries additional risk independent of the gateway chosen.

## BharatPeX offers an API but stays behind an enterprise wall

BharatPe spent its first seven years as a pure offline merchant payments company — its products on bharatpe.com are still exclusively physical: BharatPe QR, BharatPe Swipe (card POS), BharatPe One (all-in-one device), and BharatPe Speaker ([BharatPe Business](https://bharatpe.com/business)). That offline focus was not merely strategic; before April 2025, the company lacked the regulatory clearance to operate as an online payment aggregator. Its subsidiary Resilient Payments filed for RBI online PA approval in January 2023 and received it on **April 8, 2025** ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-bharatpe-rbi-payment-aggregator-approval-2025/)), launching under the BharatPeX brand shortly after.

BharatPeX.com explicitly lists **"Easy integration via API, SDK, or Plugin"** as a feature ([bharatpex.com](https://bharatpex.com)), and at GFF 2026 the company announced a full enterprise payment gateway with dynamic routing, **PCI DSS Level 1** compliance, recurring payments, split payments, and virtual accounts ([Passionate in Marketing](https://www.passionateinmarketing.com/bharatpex-launches-enterprise-payment-gateway-at-gff-2026-with-intelligent-routing-and-unified-payment-operations/index.php)). CEO Sandeep Indurkar stated at the October 2025 launch, "We don't stop at an API" — confirming an API exists ([Digital Terminal](https://digitalterminal.in/amp/story/solutions/bharatpe-introduces-bharatpex-with-ai-assistant-for-seamless-upi-and-card-payments)). The BharatPeX.AI assistant can even generate sample integration code covering UPI Autopay and UPI Circle journeys ([Newsable/AsianetNews](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/business/bharatpe-launches-bharatpex-ai-powered-digital-payments-stack-articleshow-swke0ys)).

The catch: none of that documentation is public. No API reference URL, no SDK package, no sandbox, and no developer sign-up page exists on either bharatpe.com or bharatpex.com. A site:bharatpe.com search for "developer API integration" returns zero bharatpe.com results — only third-party directories and Fiverr gigs. The API Evangelist directory, which surveys public developer surfaces, reported **no public developer API, SDK, sandbox, or OpenAPI surface** for BharatPe ([API Evangelist](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/bharatpe/)). BharatPeX's own website describes an integration model involving a "dedicated Product, Tech, and Relationship team," not a self-serve flow. This contrasts sharply with Razorpay (docs.razorpay.com) and Cashfree (docs.cashfree.com), both of which have fully public documentation and sandbox environments. There is also complete silence in developer communities: no Reddit threads, Stack Overflow questions, or GitHub repositories discuss BharatPe website integration, consistent with an enterprise-gated onboarding model rather than a public developer program.

## Cashfree has the clearest — and most conditional — gaming policy

Among Cashfree, PayU, and EaseBuzz, **Cashfree is the only gateway with a publicly documented, explicit carve-out for online gaming merchants**. Its onboarding FAQ lists "Gambling, lottery tickets, and online betting" as prohibited but **online gaming (non-money) as permitted** — with the requirement of an RNG certificate (if applicable) and a legal opinion ([Cashfree Onboarding FAQs](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/help/onboarding-related/onboarding-faqs)). This distinction matters: a business selling game-related digital goods is far closer to "online gaming" in Cashfree's framing than to "gambling," provided the merchant can articulate this clearly and produce the required legal opinion. Cashfree also requires a live website with visible contact details, privacy policy, refund policy, and terms pages before activation — standard e-commerce requirements.

There is a meaningful catch specific to account-credential businesses: Cashfree explicitly prohibits **"counterfeit, unauthorised, or copyrighted goods, including media and software"** ([Cashfree Onboarding FAQs](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/help/onboarding-related/onboarding-faqs)). A compliance reviewer at Cashfree could classify the resale of game account credentials under this category, particularly because most major game publishers (Steam, Riot, Activision) prohibit account transfers in their Terms of Service. This risk is real and must be addressed proactively — ideally by framing the business explicitly as a peer-to-peer resale marketplace and by having a legal opinion ready that addresses Indian law compliance for such sales.

## PayU and EaseBuzz leave key questions unanswered

**PayU** references a "Schedule B" list of banned merchant categories in its Merchant QR GTC, and clause 7.2.29 imposes liquidated damages equal to six months of Customer Charge for violations ([PayU Merchant QR GTCs](https://payu.in/bfl-payu-merchant-qr-gtcs/)). The full Schedule B is not publicly available — it must be requested directly from PayU's merchant onboarding team. PayU has also had recent regulatory turbulence: the RBI returned its payment aggregator application in 2023 and granted only in-principle approval in November 2024 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/industry/banks/story/payu-gets-rbis-in-principle-nod-to-operate-as-payment-aggregator-after-a-year-long-wait-426648-2024-04-24)), suggesting its onboarding process may not yet be fully stabilized for edge-case merchant categories. Research found no community reports of PayU specifically rejecting gaming merchants, but absence of reports is not confirmation of approval.

**EaseBuzz** is the most accessible option on paper: it describes itself as supporting digital goods, physical products, memberships, and event tickets ([YourStory on EaseBuzz](https://ts.yourstory.com/mystory/776ea34cb0-easebuzz-an-easy-way-to-sell-online-and-collect-payments)), and has no published prohibited-categories page — meaning no explicit barrier was found. It is RBI-authorised and positions itself at small and medium businesses. The absence of a prohibited list is a double-edged signal: it reduces visible friction, but it also means there is no documented basis to confirm eligibility for gaming or account-credential sales specifically. EaseBuzz's actual policy on this category remains unknown without direct contact.

## Regulatory pressure and account-credential risk apply across all gateways

India's 2025 Online Gaming Act and MeitY's directive to banks and payment firms to block offshore betting have pushed all major Indian payment aggregators — including Razorpay, PayU, and Cashfree by name — to tighten scrutiny of gaming-related merchants ([Sakshi Post / RMG ban impact](https://www.sakshipost.com/news/real-money-games-ban-affects-payment-gateways-may-face-rs-30000-crore-hit-transaction-volumes)). Paytm was separately fined over links to online gambling ([Sigma World](https://sigma.world/news/paytm-fined-in-india-over-link-to-online-gambling/)), a visible example of the compliance exposure that makes gateways cautious. The regulation targets real-money gaming (wagering), not account marketplaces — but the **optics of a gaming-adjacent business create additional compliance friction** regardless of which gateway is approached.

Beyond regulatory risk, selling game account credentials carries structural characteristics that gateways specifically watch for: **high chargeback potential** (buyers dispute transactions; original account owners reclaim accounts) and possible classification under "unauthorised goods" due to publisher ToS violations. These are not hypothetical; they are factors that a gateway's risk team will evaluate during merchant due diligence under the RBI's PA framework. No single gateway publicly addresses this specific business model, and none can be confirmed as "safe" without direct merchant onboarding conversations. The practical recommendation from research is to apply first to Cashfree (most transparent documented path) and EaseBuzz (most broadly permissive on digital goods) in parallel, prepare a legal opinion on the business model's compliance with Indian law, and be ready to articulate why the business does not constitute gambling, RMG, or resale of unauthorized goods.

## Conclusion

BharatPeX is a real and technically capable online payment gateway, but it is not meaningfully accessible to most e-commerce developers in 2026 — it functions as an enterprise product behind a sales wall rather than a developer-facing platform. For a small gaming or digital goods merchant in India, BharatPeX is not a practical near-term option, and its payment method coverage does not offer a meaningful advantage over Razorpay or Cashfree that would justify the onboarding complexity.

The deeper challenge for a game account credentials business is not which gateway to choose but whether any gateway's compliance team will approve the model. Cashfree's explicit "online gaming permitted with legal opinion" carve-out makes it the strongest documented path, but the "unauthorised goods" prohibition creates a real risk specific to account resale. EaseBuzz represents a lower-friction alternative worth pursuing in parallel, precisely because its policy ambiguity could work in the merchant's favor. Regardless of the gateway, investing in a legal opinion that clearly defines the business as a digital marketplace — not a gambling platform or unauthorized goods reseller — is the single most useful step before any onboarding application.
